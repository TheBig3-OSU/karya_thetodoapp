Karya · Design brief

The people who have your back

A working brief on what vouching is actually for — and the questions we need to answer before we build more of it. This is a brainstorm, not a spec. Poke holes. Bring ideas.

You have people who've got your back. They're here to watch you grow, they help you however they can, and they push you toward yourself — toward your goals.

The north star, in Sasank's words

That's the whole app in one sentence. The trouble is that it's warm and vague, and warmth doesn't survive contact with real screens on its own — it has to be built into specific choices. Below: a quick primer (skip it if you already live this), the one tension everything hangs on, and four open questions. The questions are the point. Argue with them.

The 20-second primer

+ Karya is a small-group accountability app. You and a handful of people — your house — set tasks and show up for each other. Tasks earn XP, but the point is momentum, not points.

+ A vouch is the core social act: when you finish something, a friend who witnessed it can vouch — a peer's word that you actually did it. Today it shows up as a green "Vouched" badge with a little shield.

One tension underneath everything

"Have your back" and "push you" pull in opposite directions.

Having someone's back is warm and unconditional. Pushing them is pressure. The entire design job is making the pressure feel like it comes from allies, not judges — and you can't just declare that, you have to engineer it. Three things decide which way it tips: who starts the nudge, the tone of the words, and — most of all — what the app does when someone falls short.

Four open questions

The vouch

Is a vouch an audit, or a hug?

Right now it reads like a verification stamp — a shield, the word "Vouched," a system-green check. But a vouch isn't a system confirming a fact; it's a friend saying "I saw you. I'm proud of you." That's endorsement, not audit — which argues for a handshake over a shield, and warmer words than "Vouched" / "Needs vouch." The trap: warm slides into cutesy fast, and adults trying to change their lives bounce off cute. We want sincere, not saccharine.

For the group: What does a vouch actually say? Give me the real words a friend's vouch should show — and the words that would make you cringe.

The missMost important

What happens on the day someone doesn't do it?

This is the one we haven't designed at all, and it matters most. Every accountability app is good at celebrating wins. "Have your back" only becomes real on the day someone misses. Today a missed task just… does nothing. Does it go quiet? Show a red mark? Or does it reach a friend — "hey, you didn't get to the run, everything okay?" If we punish the slip or ignore it, we break the entire promise at the exact moment it counts.

For the group: When your friend misses something they signed up for, what do you — the person with their back — want to be able to do in that moment? Design that.

Micro → macro

How does a daily check-off become "growth"?

Vouching is micro — one task, one day. "Help you grow, walk toward your goals" is macro — months. Nothing today connects the two. Unless a daily win visibly ladders into something bigger a person set for themselves, "growth" is just a nice word on the box.

For the group: What's the bigger thing a daily win should add up to — and how would you want to see it filling up over time?

The container

A feed, or a room?

We built an activity feed. But a feed is a social-network shape — you scroll, you glance, you compare. A crew that has your back might want to feel more like a shared room or a group thread — somewhere you act for each other, not just watch each other. Not saying we rip it out; asking whether the container is quietly pulling us toward lurking.

For the group: When you picture checking in on your people, is it a feed you scroll, or a room you walk into? What does the right shape feel like?

Already settled — don't re-litigate

  • No watcher/spectator counts. A number like "4 people watching you" is pressure with none of the reward, and it invites comparison. The dopamine is a face and a "good job," not a tally.
  • Success gets a real theme token. The "vouched" color stops being a hardcoded green and joins the palette, so it themes with everything else.
  • The badge keeps its label. Icon-only hides a core, non-obvious mechanic — a new user can't decode a lone handshake. Direction: handshake plus a word, in a sincere tone.

Bring the version you'd actually want pointed at you.
